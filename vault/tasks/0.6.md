# hotty 0.6: a backdrop, the controls' ring, Enter commits, scroll snapping

What breaks 0.5. 0.6 began as V6-01's `scroll`, drafted and held on
2026-10-25 until margin's move to one scrolling surface was approved or
other items joined it. Others joined it, and on 2026-11-01 the maintainer
cut 0.6, picking "Cut with what's built (Recommended)" for "How should 0.6
be cut?":

> 0.6 = backdrop, focus ring, scroll snap, Enter commits, with the four
> open questions answered by their leans. scroll op, Tab-in-a-field and
> key-back move to 0.7. Left to do: spec text for two items, the four
> SDKs' backdrop flag, then the hotty-blitz 0.6.0 / hottyterm release
> chain, each tag with your go.

So 0.6 is V6-03 (Enter commits), V6-04 (scroll snapping), V6-06 (a
backdrop) and V6-07 (the controls' ring). V6-01 (`scroll`), V6-02
(`tab=program` in a field) and V6-05 (`default`) moved to
[`tasks/0.7.md`](0.7.md), still accepted, and V6-08 and V6-09, proposed,
went with them. Each item lands in SPEC.md, SDK.md and the vectors
together (AGENTS.md), on local `hotty-0.6` branches across the stack, as
0.5 did. Released 2026-11-01 across the stack.

Item template: as `tasks/0.4.md`.

### The four questions, answered at the cut

The spec legs of V6-06 and V6-07 met four questions that changed what was
accepted, or settled what it left open. The maintainer answered each by
its lean at the cut (2026-11-01).

1. **A press held when a surface becomes a backdrop** (V6-06). The
   proposal says `p` and `v` "send nothing" on a backdrop. The branch's
   SPEC says what hotty-blitz does: a press that began before ends on the
   surface, and the pointer's leave is sent. Lean: keep it. "Send
   nothing" meant new input; without the end, a press would stay down.

   **Answered** (the maintainer, 2026-11-01), by the lean: kept. SPEC
   §5.2 said so already, and a vector checks it; nothing changed.

2. **A hidden backdrop** (V6-06). SPEC makes `a=focus` `EINVAL` on a
   surface "whose placement is a backdrop". A hidden surface has no
   placement, yet hotty-blitz keeps the flag after `a=hide` and still
   answers `EINVAL`. Lean: the host drops the flag with the placement, so
   a hidden surface is no backdrop and its next placement decides again,
   as `b` belongs to the placement. Add a vector for it.

   **Answered** (the maintainer, 2026-11-01), by the lean: SPEC §5.2's
   "The placement's" and §10.1 say a surface with no placement is no
   backdrop, and its next placement decides again (44e5cfd), with a host
   vector (requires `backdrop`). The spec says nothing of `a=focus` with
   an element it has on a surface not placed, so the vector checks only
   what it does say: hidden, `a=focus` on a missing id is `ENOTARGET`,
   as on a surface never placed ("focus on a missing element is
   ENOTARGET"), where a backdrop answers `EINVAL` whatever its `t`. What
   that `a=focus` does with an element it has is left open.

3. **contenteditable's ring** (V6-07). The accepted selector,
   `:is(a[href], button, input, select, textarea, summary)`, leaves out an
   editing host, which §10.2 treats as a text field, so a focused one has
   no ring. Lean: add `[contenteditable]`, since a text field rings.

   **Answered** (the maintainer, 2026-11-01), by the lean: §8's selector
   reads `:is(a[href], button, input, select, textarea, summary,
   [contenteditable]):focus-visible`, and App. B says why (44e5cfd).

4. **The Python client's Placement** (V6-06). The branch put `backdrop`
   after `z`, which moves every positional field after it. Lean: put it
   last, so no caller breaks over a free choice.

   **Answered** (the maintainer, 2026-11-01), by the lean: `backdrop` is
   the last field of the Python client's Placement, and SDK.md's names
   (App. A) list Backdrop last in every language (44e5cfd). `b` still
   goes out after `z`.

- [x] **V6-04** A scroll comes to rest on a row: CSS scroll snapping
      status: built, released in 0.6 (2026-11-01); accepted by the maintainer, 2026-10-30 | added: 2026-10-30
      problem: a document laid out in rows of cells (a list, a file's
      lines, one row each) scrolls by the host's steps, which are not
      rows. hotty-blitz moves 40 CSS px for an arrow and 7/8 of the box
      for a page. After Page Down a pane shows half a row at its top, and
      at a cell height that doesn't divide 40 px an arrow stops between
      rows too. Seen in a two-pane file preview whose panes the host
      scrolls by the wheel and the keys: at 20 px rows an arrow is two
      rows, and a page 29¾. A document can't ask for row steps. V6-01's
      `scroll` op moves by the same steps, so it inherits this.
      proposal: hosts honour CSS Scroll Snap on an element that scrolls
      (§5.3): `scroll-snap-type`, `scroll-snap-align` and
      `scroll-padding`. A program that wants whole rows marks them
      `scroll-snap-align: start` under `scroll-snap-type: y mandatory`, and
      every scroll then comes to rest on one, as in a browser: a key's
      step, a page, the wheel, a `reveal` and V6-01's `scroll`. The
      host's own steps stay as they are; snapping is where they end.
      - Browser-like: a standard property with its standard meaning, so a
        document a browser draws scrolls the same, and nothing new is
        named.
      - Not chosen: steps of one cell row by default. It changes every
        document, prose included, and a browser doesn't step so.
      cost:
      - §5.3: the three properties, in the list of CSS that applies to
        scrolling.
      - hotty-blitz: Blitz has no scroll snapping; a patch on its pinned
        Blitz that picks the snap position after each scroll, mandatory
        and proximity.
      - The xterm addon: a browser has it already.
      - Vectors: a list of 20 px rows under `y mandatory`, after Page
        Down in a box of 30.5 rows, shows a whole row at its top.
      - Not breaking: a document without the properties scrolls as
        today.
      legs (local `hotty-0.6` branches, pushed when all had landed):
      - [x] hotty: SPEC §5.3, §16, App. B; conformance/README.md; vectors
        (1 host, requires `scroll` and `view`); CHANGES; docs (992bd38,
        282e070, 2026-11-01). The vectors never scroll by a distance, so
        the vector's box of 30.5 rows holds ten rows above a note that
        aligns nothing: a page ends in the note, past every snap position,
        and comes to rest on the last row on any host that snaps, however
        far it pages. hotty-blitz's tests/snap.rs checks the list of 20 px

- [x] **V6-06** A backdrop: a placement drawn under the cells
      status: built, released in 0.6 (2026-11-01); accepted by the maintainer, 2026-10-31: "Accept" | added: 2026-10-31
      problem: every placement is above the cells, whatever its `z`
      (§5.2). A program that shows several terminals at once, as a
      multiplexer does with its panes, can only separate them with cells:
      box-drawing lines a cell wide, or a blank gutter. What makes a
      native window read as one is under the text: each terminal a
      rounded card in its own background, on a darker canvas, with a few
      pixels of gutter, and the focused card ringed. A surface above the
      cells can't do it without hiding the text, and a terminal's text
      can't move into a surface.
      what kitty graphics already does: a kitty image placed with a z
      below −1,073,741,824 (INT32_MIN/2) is drawn under the cells'
      backgrounds. A cell with the default background is transparent
      over it, and a cell with any other background covers it. Ghostty
      draws such images in a layer of their own (`kitty_below_bg`), after
      the window's background and before the cells'. So most of this is
      there today for a program that speaks kitty graphics. What it lacks:
      - It rasterises the image itself, so it has to know the cell size
        in device pixels and draw the image again on a zoom, a display
        with another density, or a change of font. A surface is laid out
        by the host at the cells' size, in the theme's colours
        (`--hotty-*`, §8), and drawn again without the program (§5.3).
      - It sends every frame whole, where a surface sends deltas (§6).
      - It needs kitty graphics. The xterm addon has none, and a
        multiplexer would have to relay them.
      - Nothing says a host has it, short of drawing and looking.
      proposal: a placement key `b`, a backdrop. With `b=1` the
      placement is drawn under the cells, as kitty graphics draws images
      below the backgrounds: a cell with the default background is
      transparent over it, and a cell with any other background, the
      cursor and the text cover it.
      - **Stacking:** backdrops are under every cell and under every
        placement without `b`. Among themselves they stack by `z` and
        creation, as placements do (§5.2).
      - **The placement's, not the surface's:** like `z`, `b` belongs to
        the placement. Placing the surface again without `b` puts it
        back above the cells.
      - **No input:** a backdrop takes no pointer, and the pointer
        reaches what is above it, the cells or another window. It never
        has the keyboard. `a=focus` on a surface whose placement is a
        backdrop is `EINVAL`, and no click focuses it. Its hover, press
        and fit events (`v`, `p`, `f`) hold as on any placement,
        except that a backdrop sees no pointer, so `p` and `v` send
        nothing.
      - **The feature `backdrop`** (§4): a host lists it when it draws
        backdrops. A host without it ignores `b` as an unknown key
        (§15) and draws the surface above the cells, over the text. So
        a program **MUST** check `features` before placing a backdrop,
        and falls back to cells (borders, gutters) without it.
      - **The window's edges:** a host may keep room round the cells
        that no cell covers: the padding it is set to keep, and what is
        left of the window where it isn't a whole number of cells. A
        backdrop that reaches an edge of the screen goes on to the
        window's edge there. The host draws the backdrop's outermost
        pixels across that room: the edge row or column drawn out, and
        the corner pixel in a corner. So no part of the window shows the
        terminal's own background unless the backdrop or a cell paints
        it, and a program needs to know nothing of the padding. A
        backdrop that reaches no edge stops at its cells. A cell that
        paints its background still covers the room beside it where the
        host extends edge cells into the padding, since cells are above
        backdrops. Placing the backdrop in window pixels instead would
        make the program learn the padding and the window's size, which
        hotty keeps from it (placements are in cells, §5.2).
      - The rest is a placement as before: the window, the cursor
        (`C=1`), scrolling with the screen, and deletion.
      A program that cards its panes places one backdrop the size of
      the screen, at the top left with `C=1`: the canvas, and a card per
      pane in the pane's background, reaching half a gutter past the
      pane's cells, with a ring on the focused card. It leaves the panes'
      cells on the default background, and redraws the backdrop (a delta)
      when a pane moves, splits or takes the focus.
      cost:
      - §5.2: the key, the stacking and the input rules above, and "every
        placement is above the cells" becomes "every placement without
        `b`". §4: the feature. §15: nothing new.
      - SDK.md: Placement gains Backdrop, written as `b=1`, and the
        features table its row.
      - hotty-blitz: parses `b`, passes it to the embedder's place
        callback (a C ABI change), lists the feature where the embedder
        says it draws backdrops, and refuses `a=focus` on a backdrop.
      - hottyterm: surfaces are already kitty images, so a backdrop is
        one with a kitty z below INT32_MIN/2, left out of the pointer's
        hit test: about 30 lines in `hotty.zig`. The edges are Ghostty's
        renderer's: a strip of the edge pixels past each edge a backdrop
        reaches, about 100 lines with the test.
      - The xterm addon: a layer under xterm's canvas, with the default
        background transparent there, and as large as the element the
        terminal is in, padding included. The most work of the three.
      - Vectors: `b=1` round-trips in the builders. A host with the
        feature lets a press over a backdrop reach the cells, and
        answers `a=focus` on one with `EINVAL`.
      - Not breaking: a 0.5 host ignores `b` (§15), which is why the
        feature is needed.
      prototype: hottyterm's `backdrop` branch, patches 0047 to 0049
      (`mods/ghostty/backdrop-placements.md`, `backdrop-bleed.md`,
      `backdrop-feature.md`). It reads `b=1` in hottyterm before
      hotty-blitz does. It lists the feature through a shim that adds it
      to the capabilities as they pass to the program, until hotty-blitz
      lists it. The demo, `scripts/backdrop-demo.py` there, is three
      panes of text in cells on one backdrop of rounded cards, with a
      gutter between them and a ring on the focused card. It checks the
      features first, and draws the same panes with box-drawing borders
      on a host without `backdrop`, or with `--boxes`. The gutter is a
      row tall and two columns wide, which leaves the text about 7 CSS px
      of padding at a card's sides at 1.6, closer to a native window's;
      one column (`--gutter 1`) leaves about 3. Before the edges' rule,
      a 2874x1845 window at 1.6 showed the terminal's background in a
      strip 30 pixels tall under the grid and 9 wide beside it.

- [x] **V6-07** The default sheet rings controls on focus, not what holds them
      status: built, released in 0.6 (2026-11-01); accepted, (b) as drafted, by the maintainer, 2026-10-31: "Accept", lean b as drafted | added: 2026-10-31

# hotty 0.7: a secondary press

What breaks 0.6. At the 0.6 cut the maintainer moved three accepted items
here from `tasks/0.6.md` (2026-11-01: "scroll op, Tab-in-a-field and
key-back move to 0.7"), and two proposals came with them. V7-01, a
secondary press, joined them, and is what 0.7 is: on 2026-11-06 the
maintainer shipped it ("Ship them"), with hosts and SDKs that speak 0.6
too ("Speak both"). None of the three was built, and the maintainer moved
them to [`tasks/0.8.md`](0.8.md) ("OK, move them to 0.8"). The proposals
and the notes to consider went with them.

V7-01 lands in SPEC.md, SDK.md and the vectors together (AGENTS.md), on
local `hotty-0.7` branches across the stack, as 0.5 and 0.6 did.

Item template: as `tasks/0.4.md`.

## Accepted

- [ ] **V7-01** A secondary press: an element's own context menu, by the pointer, a touch or a key
      status: built across the stack, landing on main 2026-11-06 (the maintainer: "Ship them"; hosts and SDKs speak 0.6 too: "Speak both"); accepted by the maintainer, 2026-11-05: question 1, "Program, hotty-surf does the whole thing out of the box", and the other ten as their leans, "Take all ten". The direction was theirs before: 2026-11-03 ("Press with the button", then "may be secondary is actually fine. For touch devices that would be touch hold") and 2026-11-05 ("context menu support (hotty spec, secondary press). Full vertical: spec, sdks, hosts, plx integration into lua and SDK. Goal: native feel when interacting with individual hotty elements") | added: 2026-11-03
      problem: a surface hears only the primary button (§9's table:
      `press`, `click`, `dragstart`). A secondary press over a surface is
      the terminal's, as over the cells: Ghostty's context menu, or the
      program's mouse report. A program that wants a row's own menu at
      the pointer, as a file list or a notifications list offers one,
      can't open it from a right press or a touch's long press on a host.
      It has only a key or a button. In a native application, that menu
      is under the pointer, or on the focused row from the keyboard. The
      parts are built and wait on this:
      - hotty-surf declares menus (`<menu>`, `<action>`; `Surface.Menu`
        finds the innermost by an element, a cell or the focus, and
        `Surface.Pick` runs the item).
      - plx draws them as its own context menu (ctxmenu, the one its
        panes, tabs and stacks use). It opens them from a right press in
        the cells, and from shift+F10 or the Menu key on the focused
        element. On a host, with 0.6's press having no button, it can
        only open a tool's header menu (`toolMenuPress`).
      proposal: an element opts in with `secondary` in its `data-on`.
      A secondary press on it, or inside it, is the surface's, and comes
      as a `press` that says which button. **What the menu is, and who
      draws it, is the program's** (question 1). The spec carries the
      gesture, and the keyboard and focus the program already has (§10.1)
      do the rest.
      - **The gesture.** A secondary press is what the host's platform
        calls one:
        - a mouse's secondary button;
        - on macOS, the primary button with Control held, as AppKit makes
          it a context click (question 3);
        - a pen's barrel button;
        - a touch's long press (§9.1, where a long press is today
          whatever the host makes of one).

        A host recognises each as its toolkit reports it:
        - hottyterm: GTK's secondary button and its long-press gesture,
          and AppKit's context click;
        - the xterm addon: `pointerdown` with button 2, and the browser's
          `contextmenu`, which covers Control on macOS and a long press;
        - hotty-blitz is told the button and hit-tests.

        **The keyboard** opens a menu as keys, not as a press:
        shift+F10 and the Menu key (`context_menu`, §10.4) already reach
        the program while a surface has the keyboard. Neither is a key a
        focused element uses (§10.2), and the last `focus` names the
        element (§10.1). The SDKs open the focused element's menu on
        them (question 2).
      - **The event:** `press`, `t` the nearest element with `secondary`
        in its `data-on`, from the pressed element outward (absent where
        that element has no id, as §9 says of `t`). The detail is
        `{"button": 2, "area": area, "c": int, "r": int, "mods": int}`:
        - the button as the DOM numbers them;
        - the element's cells;
        - the pointer's cell (as `dragstart`'s, §9.1);
        - the modifier keys held, Control left out where it made the
          press secondary (macOS).

        A primary press is as today. Its detail has no `button`, which
        means 0. The name is §9's own: a press, beside `click` and
        `dragstart`, not a new kind (the maintainer's direction), and not
        the browser's `contextmenu`, which names a use, not a gesture.
      - **Only where asked.** Over an element without the opt-in, or
        anywhere in a 0.6 document, a secondary press stays the
        terminal's, so its own menu keeps working everywhere else. The
        opt-in is its own subscription, as `click`'s is: no `p` is
        needed (§5.2), and `p` alone never brings a secondary press.
      - **The terminal's menu, always within reach.**
        - Shift held makes any secondary press the terminal's: its
          menu, with copy and paste, even over an element that opts in,
          as Firefox does over a page that takes the menu, and as a
          terminal's Shift already overrides a program's mouse reporting
          (question 4).
        - Alt passes it through to the program, as §9.2 does for the
          primary button: on the cells (in SGR with Alt, `CSI < 10 ; <col> ;
          <row> M`), or the terminal's menu with mouse reporting off.
        - The surface hears nothing of either.
      - **It does nothing else.** No `click` on its release, no drag, no
        selection, no change of focus or of the keyboard (question 6),
        and the terminal's menu doesn't open. Its moves and release go
        nowhere. What it means is the program's.
      - **Detection and degrading.**
        - **No feature:** a 0.7 host routes the secondary press its
          platform has, as it routes clicks (question 8).
        - **A program that also speaks 0.6** gets 0.6 from a 0.6 host
          (§4), where a secondary press stays the terminal's and only the
          keyboard path opens its menus. So an SDK opens menus from the
          keys on every host, and in the cells.
        - **A host with no secondary gesture** (no mouse, no touch)
          sends none, as one with no pointer sends no click.
      - **The cells,** where no host draws (hotty-surf's text mode, plx
        drawing a tool in cells), are not this spec's. There the program
        reads the terminal's own mouse report, where a right press is
        button 2 in SGR while it reports the mouse. Shift gives the
        terminal's menu there too, by the terminal's own rule. The keys
        are the same. There is no long press. hotty-surf and plx already
        open menus so.
      - **Drawing the menu (question 1: the program's)**: as a surface of the
        program's, at a higher `z` (§5.2), at the press's `c` and `r` or
        under the focused element's `area`, as a select's list is drawn
        where a host shows none (§10.2, *Selects*). The program gives it
        the keyboard (`a=focus`, §10.1), so its arrows, Enter and Escape
        are its own. A click elsewhere gives the keyboard back, and the
        `blur` that follows (§10.1) closes it. It needs nothing new on
        the wire. What makes it feel native is the SDK's, and SDK.md says
        it:
        - **Open at once:** the program keeps the menu's document sent
          and hidden, so a press costs a placement.
        - **Fit:** open below and right of the pointer, flipping where
          the grid ends.
        - **Keys:** type-ahead, a submenu on the right arrow, and the
          Menu key or Escape to close.
        - **One at a time:** one menu open, closed on a pick, a `blur`,
          Escape, a scroll or a resize.
      questions (answered by the maintainer, 2026-11-05: question 1
      below, and the other ten as their leans, "Take all ten"):
      1. **Who draws the menu.**
         - Lean (b): the program, as a surface (above), in its kit's
           look. It is one look with the menus beside it: plx's own
           (ctxmenu on panes, tabs and stacks) and hotty-surf's kit menu,
           which the kit redesign is drawing now (MenuCard). It works on
           every host and in the cells with one code path, and takes rows
           a native menu can't: icons, a dot, a note, toggles, a preview
           column (Design's gaps of 2026-11-05). It adds no host UI.
         - Against it: the menu stays inside the terminal's grid, and its
           feel is only as good as the kit makes it.
         - (a) The program answers with items, and the host draws a
           native menu: a GTK popover in hottyterm, a DOM menu in the
           xterm addon. It is the truest platform feel: the host owns
           placement past the window's edge, keyboard and dismissal, and
           §10.7's item shape (`label`, `name`, `key`, `enabled`,
           `items`, a pick as `command`) would carry it. But every host
           builds a menu, rows are text only, and in plx it would look
           unlike plx's own menus next to it.
         - (c) both, (a) the default: the cost of (a) and (b) together.
         - So: (b) in 0.7, and (a) later as a host feature if (b) isn't
           native enough. §10.7's shape keeps the door open.
         - **Answered (b),** the maintainer, 2026-11-05: "Program,
           hotty-surf does the whole thing out of the box" (leg 5).
      2. **The keyboard: keys, not a press.** Lean keys. shift+F10 and the
         Menu key reach the program now, which knows the focused element,
         and the same code works in the cells and on a 0.6 host.
         Otherwise the host turns them into a `press` with `"key": true`
         and `c`/`r` the focused element's first cell, which costs every
         host the keys, and a program that binds them loses them.
      3. **macOS: Control with the primary button is secondary.** Lean
         yes, with Control left out of `mods`, as AppKit and browsers on
         macOS make it a context click. A program that wants a Control
         click on macOS can't have one over an element that opts in.
      4. **Shift always gives the terminal's menu.** Lean yes: Firefox's
         escape and the terminal's own Shift override. A program loses
         Shift with a secondary press (Windows' extended menu).
      5. **The token's name.** Lean `secondary`: it names the button the
         element takes, and the maintainer found it fine (2026-11-03).
         Others: `menu` (names a use the spec doesn't define), and
         `contextmenu` (the browser's event, which this isn't).
      6. **Focus.** Lean no change of focus. A browser focuses a
         focusable element on a right mousedown, but here the program
         opens its menu, and focus moving under it would send `blur` and
         `focus` it didn't ask for.
      7. **`c` and `r` on a secondary press only.** Lean yes: a menu
         opens at the pointer, and a primary press has no such use yet.
         Adding them to every press is also additive, but costs bytes on
         the commonest event.
      8. **No capability.** Lean none: it is 0.7, as `click` is 0.1. A
         feature `secondary` (§4) would only say what the version says.
      9. **The click that closes a menu.** Lean: it does what it would
         have done, as today. The host acts on it (a focus, a checkbox),
         and the SDK drops only what the program would have done with
         it. A native menu swallows that click; doing so here needs the
         host to hold a press for a surface (a modal placement), which is
         a later item if (b) feels wrong without it.
      10. **Room for more buttons later.** The maintainer would take every
          button a terminal reports, in time (2026-11-03).
          - Lean: shape `button` for that now. It is an int, left out for
            the primary, in the DOM's numbering (`MouseEvent.button`):
            0 primary, 1 middle, 2 secondary, 3 back, 4 forward, and 5 and
            up for the further buttons a terminal can report (xterm's 10
            and 11).
          - A later item then adds a button with a `data-on` token that
            names it (`middle`, `back`, `forward`), with no new kind and
            no change to `press`'s detail.
          - Why the DOM's numbering: a browser host has it as is, its
            first three match the button codes of SGR mouse reports (0, 1,
            2), and a terminal host maps X11's 8 and 9 to 3 and 4.
          - The other choice, xterm's own numbers (1 to 3, 8 to 11), makes
            the secondary button 3 and leaves a gap where the wheel's 4 to
            7 are, which a press never is.
          - The middle button stays out of this item: it is the
            terminal's, and pastes on Linux.
      11. **Can an old program get a secondary press?** Lean no.
          - A 0.6 program meets a 0.7 host at its version, and a 0.7
            program gets one only from an element it marked.
          - One case remains within a single program: code that handles
            `press` generically (a framework's dismiss-on-press, say) sees
            a secondary press from an element some other part marked.
          - The SDKs' press helpers expose the button, and SDK.md says to
            check it. No spec text is needed.
      cost:
      - §9's event table, `press`: when, "the primary button pressed …,
        or a secondary press (above) on an element with `secondary` in
        its `data-on` or inside one, without Shift or Alt"; detail, the
        `button`, `c` and `r` above.
      - §9's subscription table, a row: `press` (secondary) | the element:
        `secondary` in its `data-on` | while it has it | each press.
      - §9, *A press*: `t` for a secondary press is the opted-in element.
        §9.1: a long press on such an element is a secondary press.
        §9.2: "a press of the primary button with Alt held" becomes "of
        the primary or a secondary press", and Shift's rule beside it.
      - §3.3's typed body: `button`, an int, 2. §16: the vectors check it.
        Appendix B: why one kind with a button, why an opt-in, and why
        the program draws.
      - SDK.md:
        - Detail gains Button, C and R on press, and the names table.
        - The keys that open a menu.
        - What makes a drawn menu feel native (above).
      - HOST.md: the gestures each platform calls secondary.
      - Vectors (hosts):
        - a secondary press on an element with `secondary` reports
          `press` with `button: 2`, `area`, `c`, `r`;
        - nested opt-ins, innermost wins;
        - an element without it reports nothing, and with Shift or Alt
          nothing either;
        - `p=0` still reports it;
        - its release makes no `click`, and focus doesn't move;
        - a primary press on the same element has no `button`.

        Vectors (SDKs): decoding the detail with and without `button`.
      - Not breaking within 0.7: a document that marks nothing sees no
        change.
      legs (after the maintainer answers):
      - **Rules.** One coding agent at a time. Each leg is on a local
        branch until 0.7 is cut, as 0.5 and 0.6 were. hotty Agent owns
        and reviews each, except plx's, which plx's own agent builds from
        a brief through gov.
      1. **hotty: the text** (S–M). SPEC.md §9, §9.1, §9.2, §3.3,
         Appendix B; SDK.md; HOST.md; the vectors; the Python client
         passing them.
      2. **hotty-go** (S):
         - `Detail.Button`, `C` and `R` on press, and the `secondary`
           token among the `data-on` helpers;
         - hottytea's `EventMsg` carries them;
         - the vectors pass.
      3. **hotty-blitz** (S–M):
         - a press with its button through the C ABI;
         - hit-tests a secondary press for the nearest opted-in element;
         - no click, drag, selection or focus;
         - the vectors' host half.
      4. **hottyterm** (M):
         - **A hook in Ghostty's mouse callback** (counted lines), and
           the rest in `hotty.zig`. It routes the secondary button, GTK's
           long press and AppKit's context click to hotty-blitz before
           Ghostty's menu, where the element opts in.
         - **Shift** is Ghostty's menu, and **Alt** the program's
           report.
         - **The first host end to end:** a smoke run and a shot of a
           menu opened at the pointer.
      5. **hotty-surf: the whole menu, out of the box** (M, after the
         kit's MenuCard lands). A program declares an element's items and
         nothing more, with the API surf has today: `Node.Menu` of
         `MenuItem`s (`Label`, `Icon`, `Key`, `Disabled`, `Danger`,
         `Heading`, and `Act`, what a pick runs, as a click's handler
         does) and separators, or in a template `<menu>` with `<action
         do= icon= key= :disabled=>` and `<sep/>`. hotty-surf does the
         rest, with no wiring per program:
         - gives each element with a menu `secondary` in its `data-on`
           on a host;
         - opens the innermost menu on a secondary press, at `c`/`r`, and
           on shift+F10 or the Menu key, under the focused element;
         - draws it as a surface in the kit's MenuCard, below and right
           of where it opens, flipping at the window's edges, and gives
           it the keyboard;
         - the arrows, Home and End, type-ahead, a group's right arrow,
           Enter;
         - closes it on a pick, a `blur` (a click elsewhere), Escape, a
           scroll or a resize, one menu at a time;
         - runs the picked item's `Act`, so the program hears a pick as
           it hears a click;
         - in the cells, the same from SGR's button 2 and the keys, drawn
           in the cells;
         - in surfrun's runtime, and in a hosted `surfrun.Surface`, whose
           Frame carries the open menu as it carries a select's open list
           (`Frame.List`), so a host application places it with no menu
           code of its own.

         The felt check at the maintainer's sizes is mine.

           as they are: they are on plx's cells and decorations, which
           mark nothing, so the two never meet.
         - **The Go SDK for tools** (`sdk/tool`, `sdk/surfdk`): delete

         - **Lua (plx-script's ui):** the same `<menu>` through surflua.

## Withdrawn

- [x] **V7-02** A tooltip: an element's `title` is the host's to show
      status: withdrawn (the maintainer, 2026-11-03: "withdraw, no spec changes") | added: 2026-11-03
      why withdrawn: the maintainer asked for a tooltip as a surface, as
      a context menu is, shown after the pointer rests ("Can we make
      tooltips a surface, similar to context menu, but triggered by
      cursor move with a delay?"), and then for a hotty-surf feature
      ("tip -> title, but make it a component, not plain text, so
      tooltip could be complex. hotty-surf feature"). §9.4's `hover`
      already gives a program the element and the cell under the
      pointer, and placements stack by `z` above cells and other
      surfaces (§5.2), so a program places its own tooltip and no host
      changes. §7 stays as it is. The text below is the record.
      problem: a document can't explain a number or an icon on hover.
      HTML's way is `title`. §7's "local behaviour stays local" lists
      hover and the pointer's shape, and says nothing of `title`, so a
      host may drop it: hotty-blitz does, the xterm addon shows it,
      because its surfaces are the browser's DOM. A document that draws
      its own tooltip inside the surface (`:hover` and a delay) is
      clipped to the surface's cells, so a row near an edge, or a
      one-row surface such as a status line, can't show one at all.
      Found by a plx tool ported to hotty-surf (plx vault/feedback
      2026-11-02-surf-hover-icon-buttons-tooltips, item 4): the old tool
      explained its numbers in `title`, and its port lost them.
      proposal: `title` joins the local behaviour.
      - **A host that shows a pointer SHOULD show an element's `title`**
        as its platform's tooltip. It shows the nearest `title` from the
        hovered element outward, as a browser does, after the platform's
        dwell, and hides it when the pointer moves off, a key is
        pressed, or the surface changes under it.
      - **It may draw outside the surface**, over the cells and other
        surfaces, as a menu would. It is the host's own drawing, like a
        hyperlink's address (§7), and is never part of the document's
        picture.
      - **No round trip:** no event, and nothing to subscribe to.
      - **Touch and keys:** none. A long press is the terminal's (and
        secondary, V7-01, where asked), and focus shows no tooltip. A
        document that must explain itself to every user writes the text
        on the page.
      questions (lean first):
      - **SHOULD or MAY.** Lean SHOULD: a document can't tell whether a
        host shows it, and a tooltip it can't count on is one it won't
        use. MAY would leave hottyterm free not to, at the cost of the
        port above.
      - **Concealed text** (§11, what a reader such as `plx read` gets).
        Lean: `title` is not in it, as hover isn't. A reader that wants
        it reads the markup.
      cost:
      - SPEC §7, "Local behaviour stays local": a bullet for `title`.
        Appendix B: why the host draws it rather than the document.
      - Vectors: none. It sends nothing and changes no picture.
      - Hosts:
        - hotty-blitz: hit-tests the hovered element for its `title`,
          through the C ABI (a query the host makes on a hover's dwell);
        - hottyterm: shows it with the apprt's tooltip (GTK's
          `GtkTooltip`, macOS's `toolTip`), a hook in each apprt's
          pointer-motion path;
        - the xterm addon: nothing, the browser shows it now.
      - hotty-surf: `tip="…"` on any element, written as `title`. It can
        ship before this lands; until then only browser hosts show it.
      - Not breaking: a document without `title` sees no change.
